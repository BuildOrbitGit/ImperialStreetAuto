# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Burnaby and surrounding area residents (Royal Oak SkyTrain catchment, Metrotown, New Westminster) who need vehicle repair. They find the shop via Google search, typically on mobile, often when stressed about a car problem. They want confirmation that the shop is real, honest, and won't waste their time or money — then they want to call or walk in. Secondary: commuters near Royal Oak SkyTrain who can drop off a car in the morning.

## Product Purpose

Drive phone calls and walk-in visits to a neighbourhood auto repair shop at 5186 Imperial St, Burnaby BC. Success is a visitor picking up the phone or walking through the door.

The site must resolve the trust question fast: "Is this place legit and will they treat me fairly?" Everything — pricing transparency, real address, real hours, real reviews — exists to answer that question so the visitor takes the next step.

## Brand Personality

Honest, neighbourhood, no-nonsense. The shop has been on the same street since 2010. Not a chain, not a franchise — a real mechanic who has been in the community for years.

Emotional goal: **relief**. When someone lands here after searching "mechanic Burnaby", they should feel "I found my place."

Voice: direct, specific, confident without arrogance. Says "Honest quotes, work done right" — not "Best-in-class auto solutions."

## Anti-references

- **Franchise chains** (Jiffy Lube, Midas, Meineke): templated, impersonal, upsell-heavy, corporate. The site should feel like the opposite of a chain.
- **Greasy garage cliché**: clip-art wrenches, red/black checkered flags, "FAST! CHEAP! RELIABLE!" aesthetic — cheap and untrustworthy.
- **SaaS / startup vibes**: gradient orbs, dark dashboards, hero metrics, glassmorphism-as-default — looks like a tech company, not a neighbourhood shop.
- **Low-effort local business template**: stock photos of strangers, Wix-era layout, no real information.

## Design Principles

1. **Trust lands in 5 seconds.** Credentials (since 2010, walk-in welcome, same-day service, real reviews) must be immediately visible. A visitor shouldn't have to scroll to know this shop is legitimate.
2. **The phone number is always reachable.** Every section should leave the number accessible. On mobile, the call button is the whole point.
3. **Specificity beats polish.** Real prices, real address, real hours beat beautiful vague claims. When in doubt, add a real number, not a better headline.
4. **Industrial confidence, not intimidation.** The aesthetic can be bold and craftsman-made. It should not feel cold, corporate, or hard to approach.
5. **Walk-in frictionless.** No appointment needed is the single biggest differentiator vs. chains. That message should be unavoidable, not buried.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA. Mobile-first (many visitors are on the street, on a phone). Ensure readable contrast on blue backgrounds — the current palette uses cornflower blue sections that need careful text colour handling.
